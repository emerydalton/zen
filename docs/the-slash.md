# The slash

Written on 2026-11-13, after zazen.md.

## The rule

A Zen term in /zen always carries a forward slash in front of it.

/zen, /zendoc, /zazen, /sesshin, and any that come later.

Zen is not /zen. /zen is not Zen.

## The long way

/zen takes words from Zen. It does not take Zen. The slash is how the
page shows which one is meant. A word with the slash is a term of this
practice. The same word without it is the word as Zen uses it, and says
nothing about what happens here.

So the slash is never dropped, in any note, in the README, in the
changelog or in the wiki. The author's words were: "enforce rigorously
always and everything".

This rule reaches back. three-names.md says that when a better way is
found, the old notes are not redone to match. The author set that aside
for this one rule. An unslashed term in an old note is corrected when it
is found.

Two things cannot carry the slash, because the tools do not allow it: a
file name, such as zazen.md, and the repository's address. Those are
names of files and places. In a sentence the term is always written with
its slash.

## What was found

On the day the rule was given, every /zen, /zendoc and /zazen in the
repository and the wiki already had its slash. One term did not. /sesshin
was written without it four times, twice in loose-ends.md and twice in
zazen.md. All four were corrected.

## What could not be told

- At "Zen terms". it-has-to-serve.md has "Papañca" and "Monkey mind", and
  names Zhiyi and the Tiantai tradition. These came into the loop as the
  author's words for something, and not as terms of /zen. The reader could
  not tell whether the rule covers them, and left them as they are.
- At the slash and the commands. words-not-commands.md says the slash
  makes some tools take the words for commands, and that this does not
  matter. Now the slash has a meaning of its own. The reader could not
  tell whether that was always why it was there.

## How this was learned

zazen.md had the line "would make a sesshin a /zazen too". The author
quoted it back, and then gave the rule, the spelling /sesshin, and the two
lines about Zen and /zen.
