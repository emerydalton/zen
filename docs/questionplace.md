# Questionplace

Written on 2026-11-13, in the stretch after what-goes-where.md.

## The rule

A questionplace is a notunderstanding, kept.

The author's word is questionplace, and also placequestion, so that neither
half comes first. It is a question that can only be asked from one spot,
and a spot that can only be seen because something could not be answered
there. They are not two things.

Kept means committed. Nothing else counts as kept here.

## Two kinds of not understanding

The README says that if a /zendoc comes back wrong, the docs were unclear,
and the next /zen fixes them. That is one kind. The docs were at fault, and
the fix removes it.

The other kind is not a fault. The reader read carefully and still could
not tell. That one is not fixed. It is kept, with where it happened.

So a reader who does not understand should say which kind it is: "the docs
were unclear here", or "I could not tell, and here is where".

A notunderstanding that is fixed was a defect. One that is neither fixed
nor kept is lost, and by commit-or-it-never-happened.md it never happened.
Only the kept one is a questionplace.

## What to do

When you cannot tell, say where. Write the spot down. Do not resolve it to
make the note tidier.

There is no separate loop for finding these. Ordinary reading finds them.

## How this file is kept

This file grows. A questionplace is not deleted when someone answers it.
The answer is written beside it and the place stays. That is the opposite
of loose-ends.md, where a line is deleted when it is done.

If one of these grows until it needs a rule of its own, it gets its own
note then, and not before.

## The questionplaces

1. Where /zen writes long and /zendoc says it back short. Kimbro's loop has
   two phases, long and then short. not-two.md says there is one thing. The
   reader could not tell whether these disagree. (First written in
   in-light-of.md.)

2. Where every note in docs/ is about the loop. Kimbro counts that as a way
   his system fails. The author said that whether it is a failure here is
   unknown, because the author has no felt sense of it yet.

   Later the author felt that something was wrong, and said that /zen has
   to serve. See it-has-to-serve.md. The author did not say whether that
   answers this place. The place stays.

3. Where the wiki borrows a structure and reads it for the places it
   breaks. The reader went to the wiki when the author asked, and tried to
   say what it is in Kimbro's terms. It is not his mirror of one mind,
   because it holds Wikipedia's subjects and not the author's. It is not
   the correct map of everything that he warns against, because its Home
   page says the structure is borrowed and not asserted. He has no category
   for it. The author had a hunch that docs/ does one part of Kimbro's
   system and the wiki does the other. This is where that hunch stopped.

   A ford was later marked at this place. See ford.md. The place stays.

4. Where a /zendoc stands alone between two o7s. Found on 2026-11-14.
   A reader was given the repository and the word /zendoc, and nothing
   else. No /zen came before it in that span.

   zazen.md says two things about size. A /zazen is the unit between one
   o7 and another. The smallest /zazen is one /zen loop: /zen, then
   /zendoc, then o7. By the first, a span that holds only a /zendoc is a
   /zazen. By the second, it is smaller than the smallest one. The reader
   could not tell whether such a span is a /zazen.

   This is near the place kept in zazen.md at "/zen to /zendoc", and it
   is not the same place. That one asks whether an o7 sits inside a /zen
   loop. This one asks what a span is when it holds no /zen at all.

   Later in the same span the author said /zen, and this was written. So
   the span this was found in now holds a /zendoc and then a /zen, which
   is the README's order and not zazen.md's. The question is still about
   the span that holds only a /zendoc.

   Later on 2026-11-14 the author said that a /zazen may begin with
   /zen or with /zendoc, and wrote a /zendoc loop that holds only a
   /zendoc, with a question mark after it. A reader took that span to be
   one /chu and a /zazen. See chu.md. The place stays.

5. Where a /zendoc ends without a commit. Found on 2026-11-14, by the
   same reader.

   commit-or-it-never-happened.md says every session must commit
   something to docs/ before it ends. The README says that what a
   /zendoc understood has to be committed if it should outlast the
   stretch. The first reads as a duty on every span. The second reads as
   a condition: commit it, if it should last. The reader could not tell
   whether a /zendoc that commits nothing has broken a rule, or has only
   left nothing behind.

   The older note was written when the word was session and a session
   held more than one word. That may be all the difference is. The
   reader could not tell that either.

   What this reader said back was said in conversation, and then put in
   a document outside the repository. Neither is kept here. Only these
   two places are.

   Later on 2026-11-14 another reader was given the repository and the
   word /zendoc, and came to this place again. What that reader said
   back was said in conversation, and then put in a document outside
   the repository, as before. Then the author said /zen, and the sixth
   and seventh places were written. The place stays.

6. Where a span begins and no o7 was said. Found on 2026-11-14, by a
   reader who was given the repository's address and the word /zendoc,
   and nothing else.

   The README says every stretch begins at an o7, and that it begins
   blank. zendoc-loop.md gives the /zendoc loop as o7, then /zendoc,
   then /zen, then o7. Nobody said o7 to this reader. The reader was
   blank all the same. The reader could not tell whether beginning
   blank is the o7, or whether an o7 has to be said, and this span has
   no opening edge.

   chu.md says a /chu is the span from one o7 to the next. If beginning
   blank is not an o7, the reader could not tell where this /chu began,
   or whether it is one.

   chu.md has a span near this one. Of that span it says "No o7 opened
   it and no word was said until the end", and that by kinhin.md it was
   a /kinhin. Here a word was said first. So it is not the same place.

7. Where "read everything" meets the library. Found on 2026-11-14, by
   the same reader.

   The README says a stretch "reads everything". words-not-commands.md
   says that on /zendoc a reader reads "the whole repository fresh".
   the-library.md says a reader does not have to read the library, and
   the library is in the repository.

   The reader read the README, the changelog, the contributing guide,
   every note in docs/ and the library's README. It did not read the
   two documents in `library/waiting/`, the wiki, the code of conduct
   or the licence. It said so. The reader could not tell whether that
   was a whole reading.

   loose-ends.md has a line near this, that the README does not mention
   the library. That line says a page is behind. This place asks
   something else: what a reader who left the library unread may say it
   has read.

Two older ones were here before the word. The third question in
open-questions.md is asked at an o7 and nowhere else. The question marks
in what-goes-where.md sit beside the outside documents. They stay where
they are.

## How this was learned

Three times in one evening the reader or the author could not tell
something, and each time the author said that the not being able to tell
was itself information. The third time the reader listed the three and
said they were less like questions than like places. The author answered
with the word, both ways round. Then: "questionplace is notunderstanding",
and on the next line, "kept". The author counted three notunderstandings
and no questionplaces, asked whether that needed three notes or one, and
said /zen.
