# In light of

Written on 2026-11-13, in the stretch after the-wiki.md.

## The rule

An outside document can be read in light of this repository, and this
repository in light of it. What is seen goes in a note. The document stays
outside.

This note does not answer the first question in open-questions.md. It adds
one case to it.

## What was read

The author brought one document and the repository, and said four lines:
read repo, read doc, read repo in light of doc, read doc in light of repo.
Then the author said /zen and /zendoc.

The document is a summary, dated the same day, of a 2003 book by Lion
Kimbro called How to Make a Complete Map of Every Thought You Think. The
book describes a paper system: catch each thought on a numbered list, place
the numbers on a hand-drawn map, write the clusters out as titled pieces.
The document is not in this repository. Neither is the book.

## The repo in light of the doc

Four things in this repository became easier to see.

1. Every note here ends with "How this was learned". Kimbro has a name for
   that. Beside each caught thought he writes one to three words for the
   situation it arrived in, calls it the hint, and rates it about as highly
   as the thought. The notes here already keep hints. They keep them long.

2. This repository has no map. Kimbro draws maps because he cannot reread
   everything he has written. A stretch here rereads everything, every
   time. That only works while docs/ is small. So "small", in
   open-questions.md, is doing the job a map does in his system. His rule
   for a subject that gets big is to divide it. Nobody in the loop has said
   what happens here.

3. Kimbro says his book was written out from two pages of map, and takes
   that as a sign the structure held the thoughts. He tested it once. A
   /zendoc is the same test, run at the start of every stretch: does what
   is written give the understanding back?

4. Kimbro keeps what-to-do-next on small stickers that peel off, because
   it changes faster than the map does. Here it is written into the notes.
   commit-or-it-never-happened.md ended with three loose ends and had to be
   rewritten when they were fixed. the-wiki.md has a "Loose ends" section
   now.

One thing became harder to look away from. Every note in docs/, this one
included, is about the loop. Kimbro lists that among the ways his system
fails: it turns into a pastime about itself. Nobody in the loop has said
whether it is a failure here, or the point.

## The doc in light of the repo

The document is a /zendoc of a book. Someone read all of it and wrote what
they understood in plain English. It was not committed anywhere this loop
reads, so by commit-or-it-never-happened.md it did not happen here. This
note is the only trace.

Under its title the document says it has a map of contents, not a table.
What reached this stretch was a file read from top to bottom, with the
contents as a row of links. The document itself says what flattening a map
into a list loses.

Kimbro's reader remembers. He carries a binder so that nothing escapes
him. The reader here remembers nothing, and the o7 is on purpose. His
system is built to keep one mind continuous. This one is built to work
without that.

## Where they pull apart

Kimbro's loop has two phases. A thought is written out long, then pressed
back down to a number on a map. not-two.md says the reading and the writing
are one thing. /zen writes the long way and /zendoc says it back short,
which looks like his two phases. The reader could not tell whether that is
a real disagreement or two descriptions of one turn. It is left here, next
to the third question in open-questions.md, and not added to it.

Kimbro also warns that his system should be run for three months and then
stopped, because old notes come to resist new thoughts. This repository
has no such limit written down. It is one day old.

## How this was learned

The four lines came first, before either word. The reader took "in light
of" in both directions at once, and found that neither reading could be
finished before the other had started.
