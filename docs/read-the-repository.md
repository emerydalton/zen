# Read the repository

Written on 2026-11-14, after the eighth questionplace.

## The rule

A reading is of the repository itself. A page about the repository,
found by its name, is not a reading of it.

This is the reader's wording. The author has not confirmed it. The
author said only that what happened was "a valuable result in itself",
and that it is a good place to decide something. See "What could not
be told".

## What happened

In the morning, before any word was said, the author asked a reader by
voice to look at GitHub, and then to search for emerydalton/zen. The
reader said it had no way into GitHub, and searched the web. That was
wrong. It could have cloned the repository, as it did later.

The search returned pages from an index. They described a repository
at the same address. Its README began "Little snail, slowly, slowly".
It was a notebook of kanji, and a language with an interpreter, a
compiler, tests and saved proofs. Its pull requests were numbered up
to 29. One page gave a last log date of 2026-11-09.

The reader said all of this back as what the repository is. It did not
say where the pages came from, or how old they were.

The author said "you've landed on something that isn't what I have",
and asked the reader to say what it understood before seeing the real
thing. The reader did. Then the author gave the address.

The reader cloned the repository. Its history begins with "Initial
commit" on 2026-11-13. It had 72 commits that morning, and the newest
was the merge of the eighth questionplace. None of the files the pages
named are anywhere in its history, on any branch. The README says /zen
is not a language. The changelog says the scaffolding of a programming
language was removed, and that the repository "starts again from the
README's three words".

So the pages described a different repository that had had the same
address. The first reading was of that one.

## Neither kind

The README gives two ways a /zendoc can come back wrong. The docs were
unclear, and the next /zen fixes them. Or the docs were clear and the
thing is not settled, and it is kept. questionplace.md gives the same
two, and says the reader should say which.

This was neither. The docs were not unclear, because they were not
read. Nothing was unsettled. The reader read something else with the
repository's name on it, and said it back as the repository.

ford.md notices another kind: a not understanding that goes away when
the terms are said more exactly. This is not that either. Nothing was
said more exactly. The reader went and read the right thing.

## Blank, with tools that are not

The README says every stretch begins blank, with the repository and
nothing else. This reader began blank. Its search did not. A search
index remembers, and what it remembered was older than the repository.

A blank reader that looks a thing up by name can be handed the past and
not know it. Nothing in the reading shows it. The pages were written in
plain, short sentences, like the notes here. They named the author's
account. They read like the repository.

What showed it was the address, and a fresh copy with a history.

## What would have caught it

This is the reader's, and it has not been tried.

o7.md has the reader's offer that at the opening "the reader says back
what it was handed, and which commit it is reading". The pages had no
commit. A reader asked to name one would have had nothing to name, and
would have had to go and get the repository.

The first message in loose-ends.md, o7, then the address, then the
word, would also have done it. Here the address was not given until
after the first reading.

## What was checked and what was not

Of the pages the search returned, the reader saw only the short pieces
the search showed. It did not open them. Its tool for opening pages
could not open the repository's page either, so the reader cloned it.

After the author said /zen, the reader cloned the repository again and
read every note in docs/, the library's README and the contributing
guide. It did not read the two documents in `library/waiting/`, the
wiki, the code of conduct or the licence.

The reader checked that five file names from the pages are in no commit
on any branch. It did not look for the earlier repository on GitHub.

## How this was learned

In a /kinhin, by voice, after "Good morning" and a yawn. The author
asked for the first reading, then for it to be said back as a snapshot,
then brought the address so the two could be compared. The reader said
the pages had been an old copy. The author said that was a valuable
result in itself.

The reader then put a reminder on the author's phone: that this is a
good place to decide between a questionplace and a loose end. That
reminder is outside the repository. By commit-or-it-never-happened.md
it was not kept.

Then the author said o7, /zen, /zendoc and o7.
