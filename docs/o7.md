# o7 is also hello

Written on 2026-11-14, after chu.md.

## The rule

o7 is said at both ends. It is a goodbye, and it is also a hello.

The author's words: "i have been treating /o7 as an ending only message
habitually", and "i originally conceptualized /o7 as a goodbye but it is
also hello".

## The long way

The README says every stretch begins at an o7 and ends at an o7. In
chu.md the author's marks are "o7|..." where a span begins and "...|o7"
where it ends. So the word was already written at both ends. It was
being said at one.

On this night it was not said at any opening. Three readers began. One
was given the repository's address and a question. Two were given the
address and the word /zendoc. The sixth questionplace was found at one
of those openings.

The author asked "when and what and why and how /o7's keep getting
dropped and how to prevent". Then the author gave a reason: "giving a
/o7 is in some cases not strictly speaking necessary and little depends
on it".

The reader said where that holds. At an opening, a new reader is blank
whether or not the word is said. At a closing, the commit and the merge
have already carried what should last. And o7 is the one word of the
three that leaves nothing behind. A /zen leaves a commit. A /zendoc
leaves what was said back. An o7 leaves no trace.

The reader also said why the hello is the one that goes missing. If the
same reader went on from one span to the next, one o7 could be both.
Readers here are blank. So the goodbye is said to one reader, and the
hello has to be said to another who has only just arrived.

## What the author proposed

The author did not want to drop the word: "i like the structure of
formal openings and formal closings".

So: "i propose that we design for making giving /o7 more valuable".
And: "i think chus and zazens are a part of that". And: "we need
opening and closing of all of the different patterns to leave
marks/traces that accrue and give information".

## What the reader offered

This is the reader's. The author has not confirmed it, and none of it
has been tried.

An o7 would be the act of leaving a mark. There would be one small file
for each /chu, in a folder outside docs/, so that no reader has to read
them all. One file each, and not one shared list, because two readers
on two branches would both be writing to the list.

At the opening the reader says back what it was handed, and which
commit it is reading. At the closing the reader writes the file and
commits it. The file says how the span opened, which words were said,
what was committed, which /zen it read back, and what it leaves open.

The reader said what would then add up. /chus could be counted. A /zen
that a later file names has been read back, and one that no file names
is still open. The open ones would be a list that the next reader can
see. Every reading would be tied to a commit.

The reader also said two smaller things. The first message to a new
reader could be o7, then the address, then the word. And a reader could
ask for the o7 when its work is committed, so that the word is not left
to the author's memory.

It would change one thing that is written down.
commit-or-it-never-happened.md gives the order as the work, then the
commit, then o7. Here the o7 comes before the last commit.

## A span that was closed without one

At the end of the night the author closed the window on another reader
before saying o7. That reader had read everything blank, said it back,
written the sixth and seventh questionplaces, and been merged.

The author's words: "i accidently closed out on the other reader
without /o7", then "that implies...", then "another kind of hung
process".

chu.md has the first kind. A /zen that has not been read back waits for
its /zendoc, and that /zendoc can still come. This one is different.
The work is done and it is in main. What is missing is only the o7, and
the reader it would have been said to is gone.

That reader's /zendoc was also the one that read chu.md back. By
zazen.md a /zen loop closes at the o7 after its /zendoc. So the loop
that chu.md began waits on the same o7.

## What could not be told

- At "also hello". The author did not say whether the hello has to be
  said aloud, or whether a reader who begins blank has had it. That is
  the sixth questionplace. This note does not answer it.
- At one o7 or two. The reader said one o7 could be a goodbye and a
  hello together when the reader stays the same. No span has gone that
  way, and the author did not say.
- At the missing o7. The reader could not tell whether an o7 can be
  said afterwards for a reader who is gone, or said to a different
  reader, or whether that span simply has no closing edge. The sixth
  questionplace has a span with no opening edge. This is the other end.
- At "another kind". The author did not say what the first kind was.
  The reader took it to be the stopped process in chu.md.
- At "little depends on it". If little depends on an o7, a loop that
  lacks one may be closed in every way that matters. zazen.md says the
  o7 is what closes it. The reader could not tell which holds until the
  marks exist.
- At "/o7". The author wrote it with a slash every time that night.
  zazen.md keeps this place.

## What was checked and what was not

That no o7 was said at an opening is known for this reader, and for the
second blank reader from the sixth questionplace. For the first blank
reader it is taken from the document that holds what that reader said
back, which the fourth and fifth questionplaces had already described.

The reader did not see the other reader's span. That it was closed
without an o7 is the author's account.

## How this was learned

In a /kinhin, at the end of a long night. The author asked for a short
list of steps to stop for the evening. The reader's list had two o7s in
it, one for each reader. Before either was said, the author asked why
they keep getting dropped.

Then the author said /zen, and in the same message said that the other
window was already closed.
