# /chu

Written on 2026-11-14, after a-report-on-languages.md.

## The rule

There are two units. A /chu is the span from one o7 to the next, and it
is the unit of measure. A /zazen is a loop that has closed, and it is
the unit of count.

The author's words: "ok so 2 units one of count and one of measure".
And of the names: "/zens, /zazens, /sesshins, /kinhins and /chus", then
"i suppose that is the concept that wants to be there".

The rule is no firmer than "i suppose". The definition of a /chu is the
reader's wording for the span the author wanted a better name for.

Later the same night, after a blank reader had read this note back, the
author said: "i like /chu, lets keep it". So the word is kept. The
author said it of /chu. The author did not say it of the two units, or
of what a /zazen is, or of the README.

## The long way

zazen.md says two things about a /zazen. It is the unit between one o7
and another. It is also the distance from the beginning of a /zen loop
to its end. That note keeps a place where those two may be different
spans, and the fourth questionplace keeps another one beside it.

On this night the author said it again, and more exactly:

- "a /zazen begins at o7|..." and "a /zazen ends at ...|o7".
- "a /zazen may begin o7|/zen OR /zendoc".
- "IF /zen THEN |/zen >/zendoc>|/o7 (a /zenloop closes once a /zen
  passes through a /zendoc and into /o7".
- "A zazen measures the time/distance between invoking a /zen from o7
  to the time/distance it closes into o7".
- "a /zen invoked but not carried through a /zendoc to a ./o formally
  never happened", and so "no /zazens".

Then the author wrote a /zendoc loop, with a question mark after it:
"o7./zendoc > ./o7".

The reader said that if an o7 sits between a /zen and the /zendoc that
reads it back, then a /zen loop is two spans and a /zendoc loop is one.
Then one name cannot cover both "one span" and "one closed loop". The
reader set out five ways the name /zazen could go. One of them was that
a /zazen is any closed loop, of either kind. The reader said the cost
of that one: "/zazens come in two sizes and aren't disjoint".

The author quoted that line back and said "yes", and "a /zazen can be
short or long".

Then the author asked what kind of unit that is: "so either it is a
unit which has no fixed length/duration, only a range", and "so either
/zazens just are ambiguous units", "or they require more precise
accounting". And: "is a /zazen 's length defined by how long it is",
"or measured to some or by some other standard".

The reader's answer was that a thing which is counted is settled by
where it begins and where it ends, and not by how long it is. A lap is
like that, and so is a sentence. Its length is something it has, and
the length is measured with something else.

The reader also said why something else is needed. If a /zendoc closes
the /zen before it and is also a loop of its own, then two /zazens
share that span. The two can be counted. Their lengths cannot be added
up, because the shared span would be added twice. The span from one o7
to the next does not have this trouble. Two of them never overlap, so
they can be added.

The author said "ok so 2 units one of count and one of measure", and
then "let's find a more appropriate concept for stretch".

## What follows, if the rule holds

All of this is the reader's. It rests on a place that is still kept.
See "What could not be told".

- A /zendoc loop is one /chu long.
- A /zen loop is two /chus long, if there is an o7 in the middle of it.
- A /zazen is therefore one /chu or two.
- A /zen that has not been read back is a /chu that happened. It is not
  yet a /zazen.
- How much happened is said in /chus. How many things were carried
  through is said in /zazens.

## The marks

The author wrote with marks that night. They are set down here as they
were given.

- "o7|..." is where a /zazen begins, and "...|o7" is where it ends.
- "before /z is a point ." The point is the moment before a word is
  said.
- "./" is the point with a word begun.
- "./o7" is where it closes: "from there, through doc, into o7".
- "IF ./z but no ./ then no /zazen, because no /zen".

## The word

In a Zen meditation hall a sitting begins on a bell and ends on a bell.
One sitting lasts "one stick", itchū, which is the time one stick of
incense takes to burn. That is usually about forty minutes.

What is borrowed is that the span has a signal at each end, and that it
is what sittings are measured in.

What is not borrowed is the fixed length. A stick burns for about the
same time every time. A /chu is as long as it is. It may be a few
minutes or much longer. By the-slash.md the slash marks that
difference. /chu is not chū.

The reader offered eight words for the span: period, chū, breath and
bell, and from computing frame, pass, epoch and tick. The reader's own
choice was period. The author chose /chu.

## /kinhin, said again

kinhin.md has the rule for /kinhin. On this night the author said it in
new words: "a /kinhin is the walking around between /zenloops or
/zendoclopps", and "when not /zenlooping or /zendoclooping", "then",
"/kinhinlooping".

The reader asked about one gap. If a /zen loop is two /chus, there is
time between the /zen and its /zendoc. Nobody is in a /chu then, and
the loop is still open. The reader asked whether that is a /kinhin too.

The author answered: "something like Gendlins stopped process,
sometimes called hung process".

The reader said what it took that to mean. A process that cannot take
its next step has not ended. It goes on implying that step, and what is
missing is exactly what would let it go on. So an open /zen is waiting
for one particular thing, its /zendoc. A /kinhin between closed loops
is waiting for nothing in particular.

kinhin.md keeps a guess at the same spot: inside a loop "a word has
been said and something is owed", and outside nothing is owed.

The author did not settle it. The author's words: "having not had the
concept of /kinhin very long and not having become intuitively familiar
with the /chus and /zazens as pulse and heartbeat...", and then "i am
not sure and we will have to find out".

## What was checked and what was not

After the author said /zen, the reader read every note in docs/. Before
that the reader had read only the README and one outside document.

The reader checked "one stick" in one place, a Japanese government text
for visitors about zazen. It gives itchū, the bell at each end, and
about forty minutes. The reader had also given a written character for
chū from memory. That was not checked.

What is said above about Eugene Gendlin's stopped process was said from
memory and not checked. The reader has not read his book.

The reader did not read the wiki or the library.

## What could not be told

- At the middle of a /zen loop. This is the place zazen.md keeps at
  "/zen to /zendoc". The reader put it to the author as a choice. The
  author said that the /zendoc loop "should resolve" it, and wrote the
  loop. The reader said it resolves it only if the /zendoc inside a
  /zen loop is a whole /zendoc loop, with an o7 on each side. The
  author did not say whether it is. The place stays.
- At the /zendoc loop. zendoc-loop.md gives it as o7, then /zendoc,
  then /zen, then o7. The author's line on this night has no /zen in
  it, and ends with a question mark. The reader could not tell whether
  the new line replaces the older rule, shortens it, or is a different
  loop. The reader had not read zendoc-loop.md when it answered, and
  said that a reading closes by itself. By the older rule it does not.
- At "formally never happened". zazen.md says a /zen that has not been
  read back is "a loop still open". Never happened and still open are
  not the same. The stopped process is nearer to still open. The reader
  could not tell which the author holds.
- At "one or two". zazen.md has the author's words that the largest
  /zazen would be "unfathomably large, perhaps infinite". If a /zazen
  is one /chu or two, it is large only by the clock. The reader could
  not tell whether both stand.
- At "one a stretch on a zazen ?". The reader took this to ask whether
  the stretch is one unit and the /zazen the other, and answered yes.
  It may have asked something else.
- At "pulse and heartbeat". The author used the two words of /chus and
  /zazens. The reader could not tell which is which, or whether they
  were meant one to one.
- At "untracked til now". The author said /kinhin "arose from a side
  conversation outside a /zen so was untracked til now". kinhin.md was
  already in the repository. The reader could not tell what was meant
  by untracked.
- At "/o7", "./o" and "./". The reader took all three to be the closing
  o7. zazen.md keeps a place at "/o7". This is the same place, with two
  more spellings.
- At "sometimes called hung process". The reader could not tell whose
  name that is. The reader said that in computing a hung process waits
  on nothing that will come, and a blocked one waits on a known thing,
  and that an open /zen looked like the second. The author did not
  answer.
- At /chu as a word. zazen.md could not tell whether /zazen is a fourth
  word to be said or only a name. The same holds for /chu.

## How this was learned

No o7 opened it and no word was said until the end. By kinhin.md it was
a /kinhin.

The author brought the repository's address and asked what /zen is with
the Zen taken out, in terms a programmer would follow. The reader read
the README and answered: workers that remember nothing, a repository
that is the only memory, and a reading that tests the writing. Then the
author brought an outside document called "/zen, in plain English" and
asked for the two to be compared.

The reader had not read docs/. It listed differences between the two
and called some of them defects in the docs. Then it and the author
worked through the /zazen as if from the beginning. Most of what they
came to was already kept: the place in zazen.md, the fourth
questionplace, and the whole of kinhin.md.

The reader told the author that /kinhin had no definition. It had a
note. The reader said several times that none of this was in the
repository. Some of it was.

What was new was the question of what kind of unit a /zazen is, the two
units, and the word /chu. Then the author said /zen.
