# The library

Written on 2026-11-14, after a-line-of-inquiry.md.

## The rule

An outside document waits in `library/`. The library is outside the
loop, like the wiki.

A /zazen reads all of docs/. It does not have to read the library, and
nothing in the library is a rule of /zen. What is seen when a document
is read next to the repository still goes in a note here.

## The long way

what-goes-where.md has two question marks. Both are the same case: an
outside document was read, something was seen, and the document was
kept nowhere the loop can reach. loose-ends.md counted five such
documents.

The author's words for what was wanted: "create a Document Library
structure in the repo it can live in until ready for processing".

So there is now a folder at the top of the repository, beside docs/.
It has a README and one folder inside it, `library/waiting/`. A
document in `waiting/` has been brought in and has not yet been read in
a loop. It is kept exactly as it arrived.

There is no folder for documents that have been read. By late binding
in three-names.md, it is made when the first one has been read, and not
before.

The library is in the repository, and the wiki is not. Both are outside
the loop. So "nothing is carried across an o7 but the repo" now needs
care. A document in the library is carried across, and a blank reader
still does not have to read it. in-light-of.md says "the document stays
outside". It now stays outside docs/, and not outside the repository.

## The first document

`library/waiting/nagarjuna-in-context.md`. Its title is "Nāgārjuna in
Context: An Integrated Reading of the Wikipedia Article and Its Linked
Sources". It reads the English Wikipedia article on Nāgārjuna against
the pages it links to and the sources it cites, and says where they
disagree.

Nāgārjuna is the second stop on the path in a-line-of-inquiry.md.

The author did not say the document was ready for processing. It is
still in `waiting/`.

## What was seen

The author asked the reader to "find the most distinctive resonances
between /zen and Nagarjuna's thought". The reader read the document
whole and said six things. All six are the reader's. The author has not
confirmed any of them.

1. This repository has three ways of meeting a not understanding. A
   defect is fixed. A problem goes away when the terms are said more
   exactly, as in ford.md. A questionplace is kept. The document, in
   its part on method, gives three ways that modern readers meet a
   contradiction in Nāgārjuna. Richard Hayes looks for a fallacy. Jay
   Garfield adds the words "conventionally" and "ultimately". Graham
   Priest lets some contradictions stand. The reader said these are
   the same three, and that this is the dispute of "some" against
   "all" in a-line-of-inquiry.md.

2. The document says Nāgārjuna has no single proof. He refutes one
   candidate at a time. not-complete.md says /zen is not a project of
   making anything complete, and late binding says to fix a thing when
   it is run into.

3. it-has-to-serve.md has the author's word papañca, for proliferation
   without arrival. The document says the chapter on nirvāṇa in
   Nāgārjuna's main work ends on the calming of that. It also says
   that emptiness is a corrective and not a ground, and that grasped
   wrongly it is like "a serpent wrongly held". The reader said this
   speaks to the second questionplace.

4. questionplace.md says of questionplace and placequestion that
   neither half comes first. The document says that for Nāgārjuna a
   cause cannot exist without its effect, and that a means of knowing
   and what it knows establish each other.

5. A reader here begins blank and has only the repository. The
   document describes a self that is a stream of events with nothing
   that carries over, and says responsibility still holds in it. The
   third question in open-questions.md asks whether a writer and a
   later reader make two. The document says the Buddha is neither the
   same as his parts nor separate from them.

6. One place where they pull apart. The README says "Not two. One."
   The document says Nāgārjuna rejects all four corners, and "one" is
   among them.

## What was checked and what was not

The reader read the whole document, and every note in docs/. Three of
the notes, zazen.md, zendoc-loop.md and words-not-commands.md, were
read in full only after the six things had been said.

The reader did not read any of the document's sources. So everything
above about Nāgārjuna, Hayes, Garfield and Priest is the document's
account, as the reader understood it. The document says of itself that
it reached several sources secondhand.

The reader did not read the wiki.

a-line-of-inquiry.md says of Nāgārjuna's four corners and of Priest's
book that they were said from memory. The document speaks of both. That
is one more secondhand account. It is not a check.

## What could not be told

- At "ready for processing". The author did not say what processing
  is. The reader took it to mean being read next to the repository in
  a loop, with a note written. It may mean something else.
- At the title. three-names.md says a note holds what its title names.
  This note holds the library and also what was seen in its first
  document. words-not-commands.md says a /zen writes one thing. The
  reader could not tell whether this is one thing or two, and wrote
  one note.
- At the ford. ford.md says a ford is a place where the repository and
  something outside it met, and something crossed. The reader could
  not tell whether this is the second ford. The document was read and
  six things were said. But the author has not confirmed them, and the
  document is still waiting. ford.md's list was not changed.
- At the wall. pointed-outward.md says the subject goes in the wiki.
  Now there is a second place outside the loop, and it holds a
  document about a subject. The reader could not tell what goes in the
  wiki and what goes in the library.
- At "One". The sixth thing may be a real disagreement, or the README
  may be saying "one" loosely. not-two.md says "They are one." The
  reader could not tell.

## How this was learned

The author brought the document and the repository's address, and no
other words. The reader read both and asked what to do with them. The
author chose two things, in the words given above: add the document,
with a library for it, and find the resonances.

The reader made the library on a branch and said the six things in
conversation. The reader said that nothing was committed yet, and that
by the repository's own rule none of it would be kept. Then the author
said /zen.
