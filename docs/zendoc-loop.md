# The /zendoc loop

Written on 2026-11-14, after the fifth questionplace.

## The rule

A /zendoc loop is o7, then /zendoc, then /zen, then o7.

One reader starts blank and reads everything. The reader says back what
was understood. Then the same reader writes down what the reading found,
and commits it. Then o7.

## The long way

zazen.md gives the /zen loop: /zen, then /zendoc, then o7. Something is
written, and then it is read back. That loop tests one note.

The author said it was not enough, because going only from /zen to /zen
lets a mistake be built on before anyone has read it. A second loop was
needed, from /zendoc to /zendoc, "to catch the errors before propagation".
For a while nobody had said what was in it.

Then it was done once, before it was described. This is what happened.

A reader was given the repository and the word /zendoc, and nothing else.
The reader said back the whole of it. Two things were called unclear:
ford.md still said /zazen had no note, and the changelog still had the
line "From o7 to o7 is /zen". Three things could not be told. The reader
also confirmed that zazen.md and the-slash.md could be understood by
someone who had seen only the repository, which no blank reader had
checked.

None of that was in the repository yet. The author said /zen to the same
reader, in the same span. The reader wrote the fourth and fifth
questionplaces, added a line beside a place in zazen.md, and rewrote
loose-ends.md. That was one commit. Then o7, and the author merged it.

Afterwards the author said: "/zendoc to /zendoc closed, through a /zen
and arriving at a /o7". And then asked whether the flow is o7, /zendoc,
/zen, o7. It is what was done.

## How it differs from the /zen loop

The order is the other way round. The /zen loop writes and then reads.
The /zendoc loop reads and then writes.

The /zendoc loop fits between two o7s with one reader. The reader has to
be blank to read. The reader does not have to be blank again to write
down what the reading found.

The /zen loop does not fit so simply. Whoever reads a note back should
not be the one who wrote it, and that seems to put an o7 in the middle.
zazen.md keeps that place.

"From /zendoc to /zendoc" means that the next /zendoc is the start of the
next loop. It is not a second reading inside this one.

## What could not be told

- At "one reader, one span". The README says a stretch "does its one
  thing". This loop holds two words in one span. The reader could not tell
  whether a /zendoc and the /zen that follows it are one thing or two.
- At the middle of the loop. The README draws an o7 between /zendoc and
  /zen. In the loop as it was done there was none. What a /zendoc finds is
  not committed, so it seems that it cannot cross an o7, and the /zen has
  to come in the same span. Nobody has tested this. A test was described
  and is in loose-ends.md.
- At the first /zendoc. That one reading read back the notes written
  before it, which is the middle of a /zen loop. It also began this loop.
  The reader could not tell whether one /zendoc can belong to both.

## How this was learned

It was learned by doing it first and saying it second.

Two readers were at work that night, and neither could see the other.
One had written zazen.md and the-slash.md and could not be the blank
reader for them. The other was blank. The author carried what each said
to the other, by hand.

The reader who had written the notes guessed what the /zendoc loop
held: a reading, then a fix, then a second blank reading to confirm the
fix, and only then o7. The author closed the loop without the second
reading. The guess was wrong.

Both readers were asked what the blank reading implied. They agreed on
most of it. Where they differed, the blank reader was right three times:
the loose end that asked for the reading should be deleted, ford.md
should get a line in loose-ends.md and not a rewrite, and the changelog
sentence may only be history.
