# Logic and contradiction

Written on 2026-11-14, after reading the two documents in `library/waiting/`.

## The rule

A report can sharpen a question without settling it.

## The question

a-line-of-inquiry.md keeps the reader's question: when a contradiction is allowed to stand, what stops it from making everything true, and is the answer a matter of logic or of whom the statement is for?

The two waiting reports do not answer that question. Read beside it, they make two different parts of it easier to see.

## What the reports say

The report on Nāgārjuna sets out disagreement about how to understand the four corners and the arguments against them. It describes readings that accept some contradictions, readings that qualify claims as conventional or ultimate, and Hayes's claim that some arguments rely on equivocation. The report does not decide among them, and says that several sources were reached secondhand.

The report on made languages distinguishes minimal sets by what they are meant to do. It says that human-language and machine-language primitives solve different problems, while logic is something they share. It describes wanting, feeling, knowing and judging as an additional part of human language. It also warns that claims about how a language changes thought are hypotheses, not established results.

These are the reports' accounts. Their cited sources have not been read here, and the claims above have not been checked against them.

## What meets

The first report gives the inquiry examples of disagreement about contradiction: accept some, qualify them, or reject the arguments as flawed. The second gives a proposed boundary between what human and machine languages share and what human language adds.

The reader sees a possible connection. The question may concern both what a logic permits and what a statement is for. But neither report shows that these are the same question. The language report does not say whether compassion belongs to logic's shared part or to its human addition; the Nāgārjuna report does not settle which account of contradiction is right.

This reading is the reader's. It does not confirm the reports' conclusions or resolve the question in a-line-of-inquiry.md.

## What could not be told

- Whether the inquiry's question about whom a statement is for connects to the language report's distinction between logic and human concerns. The author has not said.
- Whether compassionwisdom and heartmind belong to either side of that distinction. The author gave the words, but not their meaning here.
- Whether the disagreement about contradiction is logical, interpretive, or about what a statement is for. The reports offer different accounts, and their sources have not been checked.
