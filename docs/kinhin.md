# /kinhin

Written on 2026-11-14, after zendoc-loop.md.

## The rule

/kinhin is what it is to be in neither loop, and what gets carried back
to them.

The author's words: "/kinhin is not unrelated to /zenloops or
/zendocloops", and "/kinhin just is what it is to not be in either, and
what gets carried back to them".

## The long way

There are two loops. zazen.md gives the /zen loop and zendoc-loop.md
gives the /zendoc loop. Each begins with a word and ends at an o7.

Not everything happens inside one. After an o7 the author and a reader
can still be talking. No word has been said, and nothing is being read
back or written down. That is /kinhin.

It is one name for two things that the author did not separate. It is
being outside both loops. It is also whatever comes back in from there.

It is not outside /zen. The author said it is not unrelated to the
loops. Being out of a loop is still the practice.

Nothing in /kinhin is kept. What is said there is only said, until a
/zen in a loop commits it. So /kinhin does not cross an o7 by itself, and
the rule in commit-or-it-never-happened.md holds.

## The first one written down

After an o7 the author showed one reader what another reader, a blank
one, had said back. They compared what each thought the reading implied.
They worked out that a /zendoc loop had been closed, and how it could be
tested. The author asked what to call this kind of talk.

Then the author said /zendoc and /zen, and that was a loop again. What
was carried back became zendoc-loop.md and four lines in loose-ends.md.

## The word

In Zen, kinhin is the walking done between periods of zazen. Everyone
stands, walks in a line around the hall, and sits again. It is not a
break. The same attention is carried from sitting into walking. It is
silent, it has a set form, and it begins and ends on a signal.

What is borrowed is the position and the continuity. /kinhin falls
between one loop and the next, and the practice does not stop there.

What is not borrowed is the silence and the form. /kinhin here has been
talk, and nothing fixes its shape. By the-slash.md the slash marks that
difference. /kinhin is not kinhin.

## It is not the author's part

The reader said that /kinhin might be the name for the author's part of
the practice, the part no reader can do. The reader's reason was that a
blank reader has nothing between one o7 and the next.

The author said no: "i am not accidental or incidental to /zen or
/zenloops or /zendocloops", and "so it can't be more me-ness in the
/kinhin that gives rise to the distinction".

The author is in every loop. The author says the words, gives the
definitions, carries things between readers and merges. So more of the
author cannot be what sets /kinhin apart.

The reader had also been in a /kinhin without seeing it. The one written
down above had a reader in it, and the reader carried from it.

## What could not be told

- At the distinction. If who is present does not set /kinhin apart from
  a loop, something else does. The reader guessed that inside a loop a
  word has been said and something is owed, and outside nothing is owed:
  no blank start, no one thing, no commit. The author has not said.
- At the author's first try. Before the rule, the author said that
  /kinhin arises from "a walking around, a forgetting, a sense of not
  quite rightness unlocated", and that when this is sensed one comes back
  with a beginner's mind, not for either loop but for a third thing. Then
  the author asked what kinhin is, and gave the rule above. The rule does
  not mention the forgetting, the unlocated sense or the third thing. The
  reader could not tell whether they are still part of /kinhin.
- At it-has-to-serve.md. That note tells of the author going round,
  sensing that something was wrong, stopping, and saying "It has to
  serve". The reader thought this looked like a /kinhin from before the
  word. The author did not say so.
- At "/zenloops" and "/zendocloops". The author wrote each as one word.
  The earlier notes write "/zen loop" and "/zendoc loop". The reader
  could not tell whether the one word is meant, the way
  contemplationcomputation is.

## How this was learned

The author asked for a word for talk that is outside any loop. The
reader offered /kinhin, /mondo and /dokusan, and said /kinhin named
where the talk sits.

The author tried saying what a /kinhin is, and then asked what kinhin
is, because that would inform it. The reader said what the Zen practice
is, and that "a forgetting" did not fit it. The author then gave the
rule.

The reader's reading about the author's part came next, and the author
corrected it. Then the author said /zen.
