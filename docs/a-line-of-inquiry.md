## What the reader suggested

One question: when a contradiction is allowed to stand, what stops it
from making everything true, and is the answer a matter of logic or of
whom the statement is for?

The reader found a published dispute on that question between the two
people at the end of the author's path. It is in the journal Philosophy
East and West, volume 63, number 3, July 2013, in a special issue called
"Buddhism and Contradiction".

- Yasuo Deguchi, Jay Garfield and Graham Priest had argued that some of
  the contradictions in Buddhist texts should be accepted, and not all.
- Brook Ziporyn answered from Tiantai, in "A Comment on 'The Way of the
  Dialetheist'". As the reader understood the summary of it, he holds
  that being true and being liberating are not two different things,
  that every statement contradicts itself, and that this is why every
  statement can liberate and is true. His model is the raft, widened into
  skillful means.
- The three answered in "Two Plus One Equals One". They say he finds
  them too tame, and that even in Tiantai some things are simply false.

So the dispute is "some" against "all", and it is argued over compassion.
not-complete.md has the author saying that 84,000 is many and not
infinite. That is on the side of "some". The author's line about
afflictions, skillful means and all beings is on the side of "all". The
reader said so. The author did not answer.

The reader also named one thing to look for at each stop on the path:
the questions the Buddha set aside, among them whether the world is
finite or infinite; Nagarjuna's four corners; Zhiyi's Three Truths and
his three thousand worlds in one moment of thought; Priest's book The
Fifth Corner of Four; and a four-valued logic that Nuel Belnap made for a
computer whose sources disagree.
