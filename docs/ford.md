# Ford

Written on 2026-11-13, in the /zazen after questionplace.md.

A /zazen is the span from one o7 to the next. The author gave it that name
in this /zazen. It has no note of its own yet. See loose-ends.md.

## The rule

A ford is a place where the repository and something outside it met, and
something crossed. It is written down, so that the next reader knows the
crossing happened, and where.

A ford is a record. It is not a rule.

## The long way

The repository is inside the loop. The wiki is outside it. the-wiki.md
says so, and the wiki's Home page says so. Between them is a wall, and the
wall is on purpose.

A wall like that can still be crossed. A reader can go and look at the
wiki, and something seen there can be written in a note here. When that
happens, the place where it happened is a ford.

Ford is a noun, like o7. It names a place and not an action. The author
said it this way: a ford is a place in this system where a precisely,
intricately defined point needs elaboration. And then: to mark a ford is
to say, this is a channel.

To mark a ford is to write it down in docs/. Nothing else counts as
marked here.

## A record, not a rule

Marking a ford could have meant one of two things.

It could have been a rule: cross here, and nowhere else. Then the wall
gets stricter, and a ford is a door with a lock.

Or it could be a record: here is where a crossing happened. Then nothing
is forbidden. A later /zazen can cross wherever it finds ground, and when
it does, that is another ford to mark.

The author chose the second. The first would fix a thing ahead of time,
which three-names.md says not to do.

So there is no separate loop for finding fords. Ordinary reading finds
them, the same way it finds questionplaces.

## A ford and a questionplace

They are different in kind. A questionplace is found: a reader could not
tell something, and the place was kept. A ford is marked: something
crossed, and the place was written down.

They can be at the same spot. A questionplace can be carried forward into
a ford. The ford does not close it. By the rule in questionplace.md, what
comes later is written beside the place, and the place stays.

## A problem that went away

The reader pointed at two wordings. the-wiki.md says a stretch "does not
have to read" the wiki. The wiki's Home page says a stretch "does not
read" it. One sounds like permission and the other like a rule.

Then the author named the span from o7 to o7 a /zazen, and said it is a
unit. After that the problem was gone. Whether a /zazen reads the wiki is
only what that /zazen did. What it leaves behind is still only the commit.

The author said the problem had come from loosely defined terms, and that
it disappeared when they were said more exactly. Nothing was fixed in the
docs and nothing was kept as a questionplace. That may be a third kind of
not understanding, next to the two in questionplace.md. It is only noticed
here.

## The word

Crossing is Eugene Gendlin's word. The wiki uses it on its Most linked
concepts page, and says there that its account of him comes from a reading
report and was not checked against his paper. That is still true.

Ford does not rest on that account. It points at crossings that happened
in this loop. Only the word is borrowed, the way three-names.md borrows
Kimbro's.

## How this was learned

The author asked the reader to take the repository, the docs and the wiki
as one object, then as two, then as what could be. The reader said that
crossing could be a deliberate act with its own word. The author asked for
a name for this crossing and not for crossing in general. The reader
offered ford, gate and threshold, and the author took ford.

Earlier in the same /zazen the reader had said that loose-ends.md was out
of date about the wiki, and that the wiki had grown faster than the
repository's account of it. That was a misreading. loose-ends.md only says
that the three old pages are still there, which is true. The reader found
the mistake on reading everything again, and said so. The author said that
was not a failure, and that it is what the loop is for.
