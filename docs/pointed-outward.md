# Pointed outward

Written on 2026-11-14, after kinhin.md.

## The rule

The subject goes in the wiki. docs/ holds only what was seen at a
crossing.

This is the reader's reading of what the author pointed at. The author
did not say it in these words. See "What the author said" below.

## The long way

Every note before this one is about /zen itself. That was needed. The
loops had to be built by using them, and the only material at hand was
the loops. The second questionplace has kept this since the first night,
and it-has-to-serve.md says /zen has to serve something.

The author said, after the loops were in place: "now the machinery is
pretty much in place to do anything". And asked where it should lean
next, not as a list of things to fix, but what /zen is trying to be.

The reader answered that it should be pointed at something that is not
/zen, and that the first note ever written here had been about learning.
Then the reader named a limit. Once there is a subject, the repository
will grow past what a reader can read whole, and every loop begins by
reading it whole.

The author answered by pointing at the wiki.

The wiki already holds what /zen can point at. the-wiki.md says so. It
has 2,583 entries, borrowed and not invented. It is outside the loop, so
no reader has to read it whole. That is the answer to the limit. A
subject does not make docs/ larger, because the subject is not written in
docs/. It is written in the wiki. docs/ gets a note saying that a
crossing happened and what was seen, which is what ford.md and
in-light-of.md already ask for.

in-light-of.md says this repository has no map, and that "small" does
the work a map would do. The wiki is the map. This is the hunch kept at
the third questionplace, that docs/ does one part of Kimbro's system and
the wiki does the other.

The wiki also marks where to go. ford.md gives the author's words for a
ford: a place "where a precisely, intricately defined point needs
elaboration". The wiki marks 258 entries that sit under more than one
parent, and says which are densest. Those are places a ford could be
made.

## What the author said, and what the reader said

The author said three things: that the machinery is in place to do
anything, that it may not be going anywhere in particular, and "Look at
the wiki, i don't think you've seen it, and i think you will like it".
Then the author said /zen.

Everything else in this note is the reader's. That includes the rule at
the top, the wiki as the map, and the marked entries as places for a
ford. The author has not confirmed any of it.

The reader had read all of the wiki before, at the start of the same
night, and it had not changed. The reader had read it as something added
on at the end. The author was right that it had not been seen.

## What could not be told

- At "to do anything". The author said the machinery could do anything,
  and also that this may not go anywhere or achieve anything. The reader
  took the first as a wish for a subject. It may not be one.
- At the wall. The wiki is the one place the loop does not read. That is
  how three stale pages went unnoticed there. If the subject is written in
  the wiki, the readers who check each other through the repository never
  check it. The reader could not tell whether that is a cost to accept, or
  a thing that has to change before a subject is taken up.
- At which subject. The reader named Eugene Gendlin's paper, because
  loose-ends.md already says /zen could not be done without him and the
  wiki says its account of him was never checked. The reader also named
  the densest crossings in the wiki, such as logic and information
  theory. The author chose none.

## How this was learned

In a /kinhin, after an o7. The author asked a large question and the
reader answered it. The reader's answer contained a claim about a limit.
The author did not argue with the claim. The author sent the reader to
look at something, and the claim did not survive the look.
